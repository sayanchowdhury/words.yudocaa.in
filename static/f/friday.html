1
Raise temperature 3–4 min always first
ExerciseDurationWhy
Skipping — easy pace 3–4 min Raises core temp faster than walking — basic two-foot bounce, no double unders, no speed work. Comfortable rhythm only. Also primes calves and ankles before sumo squats and calf raises later
2
Hip hinge pattern drill 3 min RDL prep
ExerciseReps / setsWhy
Bodyweight good mornings 2 x 12 Teaches the hip hinge under zero load — exactly the pattern RDL uses. Hands behind head, soft knees, push hips back
Hip hinge with wall touch 10 reps Stand 30cm from wall, hinge back until glutes touch — trains the exact movement arc of RDL without bar
3
Hamstring prep 4 min priority region
ExerciseReps / setsWhy
Leg swings — forward/back 12 each leg Dynamic hamstring lengthening — mimics the eccentric load of RDL without any static hold. Do not force range
Kettlebell swings — light 2 x 15 Ballistic hip hinge — loads hamstrings and glutes dynamically through the same arc as RDL. Use 12–16 kg only. This is activation, not a training set — smooth and controlled, not maximum power
Towel heel slide curl 2 x 10 Lie on back, towel under heels, bridge hips up, slide heels away and back. Zero knee stress — isolates biceps femoris before loaded curls. Keep hips elevated throughout
4
Glute activation 3 min hip thrust prep
ExerciseReps / setsWhy
Glute bridges (bodyweight) 2 x 15 After a day of sitting, glutes are neurologically suppressed — this wakes them up before hip thrusts. Pause 2 sec at top
Clamshells (lying) 12 each side Fires glute medius — often underactive. Takes 90 seconds and makes a real difference to hip stability under load
5
Lumbar prep 2 min RDL protection
ExerciseReps / setsWhy
Cat-cow 10 slow reps Warms the lumbar spine before it's loaded under an RDL. Non-negotiable on Lower B — takes 60 seconds
Dead bug 2 x 8 each Lower back pressed into the floor throughout — trains the exact spinal bracing position needed in RDL. Exhale as you extend; the exhalation activates transversus abdominis, the deepest core muscle. If your back lifts off the floor you've gone too far
6
Dumbbell RDL ramp-up sets 5 min nervous system prime
ExerciseSetLoadRepsNotes
Dumbbell RDL 1 Bodyweight / very light DBs 10 reps Feel the hinge — push hips back, weights drag straight down front of thighs, soft knees
2 40–50% work wt 6 reps Slow eccentric — feel hamstring stretch at bottom. Palms facing body throughout
3 70% work wt 4 reps Dumbbells must stay close to legs — think pushing the floor away on the way up, not pulling
4 Working weight Working sets Into your 4 x 6–8 working sets
Total warm-up ~21 min
Lower B specific caution: Never skip the hip hinge drill (Phase 2) before dumbbell RDL. Going straight from skipping to a loaded RDL is the single most common cause of lower back tweaks in this type of session. Keep the skipping in Phase 1 genuinely easy — this is temperature raising, not cardio. Pre-fatiguing your calves before working sets is counterproductive.
Session order after warm-up: RDL first (4×6–8) while freshest → hip thrusts (4×8–10) → hamstring curls (3×12, 3 sec eccentric) → sumo squats (3×10) → single leg calf raises (3×15). Do not swap RDL and hip thrusts — RDL demands the most from your lower back stabilisers and should always be done when your nervous system is sharpest.
